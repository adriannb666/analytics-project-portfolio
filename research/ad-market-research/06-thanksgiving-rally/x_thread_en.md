Is the "Santa Claus rally" real?

I tested 76 years of S&P 500 data (1950–2025).

Short answer: yes, it's real. It's also fading, and almost useless. A thread:
[02_random_control.png]

2/
Santa rally = last 5 trading days of Dec + first 2 of Jan.

Average: +1.25%
Random 7-day window: +0.26%
Up in 58 of 76 years

Only 3 of 10,000 random simulations did as well (p ≈ 0.0003).

3/
The gains are packed into those 7 days: +0.18% a day vs +0.036% on an average day.

Mid-December is nothing special.
[03_day_by_day.png]

4/
Thanksgiving week:

Wed before: +0.33% (up 76% of years)
Black Friday: +0.28% (up 71%)
Monday after: −0.29% (up only 38%)
[04_thanksgiving_week.png]

5/
But it's fading.

2010s: +0.30% vs +0.32% for any 7 days
2020–25: +0.28% vs +0.40%

Three misses in a row: Dec 2023, 2024, 2025.
[05_by_decade.png]

6/
"If Santa fails to call, bears will come"?

After 17 failed Santa rallies, the next year was still up 12 times.

Santa failed at the end of 2023 and 2024. The S&P then rose 23.3% and 16.4%.
[06_santa_fails.png]

7/
Real but tiny: holding only these 7 days each year turned $1 into $2.52 since 1950.

Buy-and-hold: $411.

No reason to time it. And no reason to sell in December.
[07_only_7_days.png]
