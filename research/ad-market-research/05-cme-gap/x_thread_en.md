1/
"CME gaps always fill."

I tested it on 438 Bitcoin weekends (2018–2026).

It's true. And it's useless. 🧵

2/
CME BTC futures close Fri 5pm → Sun 6pm ET. Bitcoin keeps trading.

When CME reopens, the chart shows a gap. The rule: price comes back to the Friday close.

Example: SVB weekend, Mar 2023. Gap from 20,077 to 21,273.

3/
Yes, 97% of gaps fill.

But I drew 20,000 random lines at the same distances, random times, random directions.

97% of those got hit too.

Gaps don't fill because they're special. BTC is volatile. Everything 1–2% away gets hit.

4/
Now the trade people actually take:

Bet on the fill at Sunday open. TP at the gap, SL at the same distance the other way.

Stop hit first: 237
Gap filled first: 163

40.8% win rate at 1:1. About −0.18R per trade before fees. p ≈ 0.0003.

5/
Why? The opposite level gets touched faster.

Median time:
• opposite level: 7 hours
• gap fill: 16 hours

6/
And it got worse.

2018–21: gap filled first 47%
2022+: 35%

7/
"Always" isn't always either.

14 gaps are still open. SVB's 20,077 hasn't been touched in 3.5+ years.

8/
Takeaway: a rule can be right 97% of the time and still have zero edge.

Always compare a market "law" with what randomness gives you.
